# RVHG factory explorer
A private, static WebGL scene using Three.js 0.180.0 (vendored with MIT license).

Open `dist/index.html` via an HTTP server. All runtime assets are local. Room topology and equipment arrangement were transcribed from the owner's original 2D plan and selected navy/teal/copper reference. Coordinates are relative illustration units, not surveyed physical dimensions.

The scene has 3 roasters, 2 grinders, 3 powder packaging machines, 5 main packaging clusters and a manual work row. The oil store is elevated over the rear of the rolling room. The right-hand corridor remains open between the changing room and small parking area.

The floorplan is an illustrative reconstruction, not an engineering or safety approval.

## Illustrative motion and daylight update
- Individual rotating roaster drums and roller surfaces, visible drive wheels, packaging strokes, and packages traveling on existing conveyors.
- Five walking workers follow bounded paths in existing corridors; six stationary packing operators move their arms at the existing manual work row.
- The header motion control pauses/resumes the single simulation clock. `prefers-reduced-motion` starts paused and also disables camera transitions. Hidden tabs do not advance motion.
- Bright turquoise/sky/amber surfaces with coral accents. Room bounds and all machine counts remain unchanged.
- Static geometry remains batched; static shadow maps are reused, and walking figures use lightweight contact shadows.

Run `node scripts/validate-motion.mjs` and `node scripts/validate-motion.mjs --reduced` for geometry, equipment-count, path and motion-state checks. These use a mocked renderer and DOM; they are not GPU, frame-rate or visual verification.

## Reference-led contrast update
The owner's attached architectural diorama reference was visually inspected before this revision. Its brick-red facades, warm ivory trim, charcoal road surfaces, lime greenery and sandy backdrop now guide the palette. Production wall bodies are brick red with ivory caps; warehouse and office walls remain ivory with brick accents. Floors are neutral warm stone/olive-beige, machine frames deep charcoal/forest, metal surfaces subdued silver, and packaging accents restrained rust/mustard. Label text is ivory on charcoal and is unaffected by tone mapping.

Ambient light was reduced, cool fill removed, directional light lowered for clearer cast shadows, and Neutral tone mapping replaces the brighter filmic treatment. Motion, room bounds and equipment counts are unchanged.

## Right-hand WC and through-corridor update
The original right-hand sanitary footprint now contains four separate cubicles to the right of a continuous longitudinal aisle. Every cubicle has a partition, an inward-open door and a toilet; shared basins occupy the small recess to the aisle's left. The aisle continues through the left edge of the existing changing room into the existing lower corridor. Changing-room lockers remain; benches are moved to the right. All other room footprints, machine counts, the reference-based palette and motion remain unchanged.

The local test checks both usable aisle width and all four cubicle entrances plus the changing-room entrance against generated wall/fixture colliders. One walking worker now uses this continuous path. All dimensions are relative illustration coordinates, with no engineering or building-code claim.

## Latest brightness correction (supersedes the darker reference-led colors)
The owner reported that the reference-led revision was too dark in actual use. This revision restores luminous warm surroundings and raises ambient/exposure while keeping saturated coral walls, turquoise/blue machine casings and golden accents. Large charcoal/brown surfaces are replaced with light aqua/cream. Shadows are softer and lighter, metalness is lower without an environment map, and dark colors are reserved for small supports and readable label backgrounds. This correction is combined with the four-cubicle WC layout above; the motion and required machine counts remain unchanged.

The geometry/state test verifies zero parking-wall sections, unchanged vehicle counts/footprints and retained adjacent room walls.

## Fullscreen and unobstructed 3D viewing
The viewport has two always-reachable controls: fullscreen/return and hide/show panels. Fullscreen initially hides the sidebar, info card, navigation overlays and hints so the model can use the entire screen. Showing panels restores useful controls without leaving fullscreen. The existing motion control moves into the viewport toolbar during expansion and returns to the header on exit. The info card's close button now actually hides it, with one-click restoration from the persistent panel control; the sidebar can also be collapsed on desktop.

Native Fullscreen API is preferred, with a fixed browser-viewport expansion when unsupported or rejected. Escape, native fullscreenchange, resize/orientation changes and return to the prior panel state are handled. Camera and renderer resize without resetting orbit/zoom. Viewport UI is a separate module and remains usable when WebGL initialization fails. Run `node scripts/validate-viewport.mjs` for isolated interaction/state tests; these do not claim visual rendering verification.
